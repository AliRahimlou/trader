# Version 4.5.0 — video-aligned levels, retests, leaders and VIX; inverse-ETF short proxy; “Look left” chart

The owner re-watched the 18:11 recording frame by frame on September 22, 2026 and ordered production aligned with what it actually shows. The frame-by-frame read (a second-by-second timeline of the on-screen charts against the spoken words) established the following. He marks levels on an **NQ futures** 4-hour chart with extended hours and back-adjustment on, so his 4h candles run nearly around the clock (about six per weekday; his October 17 low is stamped 08:00 chart time). The lines are **hand-drawn horizontals**, about four in a 900-point range, placed over roughly five to six weeks of 4h history “everywhere where a price has broken through or touched more than once”: every line sits at a swing high or low and he justifies each one by scrolling back and pointing at prior touches, rejections, breakouts and retests of that price; the top line is simply “our high”. He then switches the same chart to the **1-hour** with the drawings unchanged (“we break and we trade the retest … always where price has been before”), and the retests he points at come one to three days after the break. His live example is not a continuation: price has just reclaimed the 24,973.25 line and he is looking for a **short** there (“if I was gonna go for a short play right here … we’re near a pivotal area … I’m going to anticipate a pivot”), with the direction decided by the leaders and the VIX. The leaders (AAPL and NVDA on 5-minute, MSFT on 15-minute) carry no hand-drawn lines; they run a **period-level indicator** (previous day/week/month/quarter/year opens, previous week high and mid, Monday high/mid/low, daily high/low, daily/weekly/monthly VWAP) plus a blue supply band and an orange demand box, read visually as “at supply”, “rejected”, “selling pressure”; he names four companies, shows two, and states the rule as majority buying → long, majority selling → short, mixed → no trade, never a count. The **VIX** is a 5-minute view switched to 15-minute “for extra confirmation”, with **no drawn objects**: “look left” means scrolling back two to three days to the consolidation base a spike launched from (about 21.0 on October 16) and checking with the crosshair that the current close is back at it; for a Nasdaq short he needs the VIX to “start buying” there. Nothing in the recording shows a stop, a target, a position size, an order, a time limit on a break, a numeric tolerance or a numeric quorum; those remain app choices and are labelled as such below.

Every Socrates rule change ships behind a new policy version, so **after rollout the Socrates card shows “Review updated rules”, the header shows “Review updated live rules”, and no new entry is sent until the owner accepts in the app** (see “Owner action required”). The 4H Range Reversal engine is untouched in this release: its own policy is unchanged, so its card normally reads “Selected · global Live Off” until the global Live switch is accepted again (it shows “Review updated rules” only if its 4.4.0 policy, `range-spot-execution-v2`, was never accepted). Saved purchase amounts ($15 each), market selections (BTC/USD) and the saved global Live permission are carried forward unchanged. The unchanged safety properties are listed at the end.

## Socrates rule changes

Each subsection separates the **video rule** (what the recording shows or says) from the **app interpretation** (the numbers and mechanics this release commits to, which the recording does not state).

### Afternoon four-hour bucket

**Video rule.** Standard 4-hour candles over the continuous futures session; afternoon and overnight extremes form 4h highs and lows like any other.

**App interpretation.** QQQ regular-session bars now form **two** four-hour buckets per session instead of one: 09:30–13:30 (240 minutes) and 13:30–16:00 (a 150-minute afternoon bucket emitted as a 4h bar). A full session therefore contributes two candidate extremes; an early-close session contributes the buckets its bars complete. The afternoon bucket is shorter than the morning one because Alpaca serves no overnight bars for QQQ (see “What is still not aligned”). Data-health and the descriptive 60/240-minute context accept the afternoon bar; the hourly frame is unchanged (buckets anchored at 09:30, the 15:30–16:00 half hour still dropped).

### Swing-based four-hour areas

**Video rule.** A handful of hand-drawn lines at swing highs and lows that were broken through or touched more than once over five to six weeks; “doesn’t gotta be pretty”; the swing high of the move is always a line.

**App interpretation.** Areas are no longer proposed by every 4h bar extreme. Candidates are **confirmed 4h swing pivots** (a bar whose high exceeds its neighbours on both sides, or whose low undercuts them), each proposing a band of **±0.1%** around the pivot price. A candidate is established when at least **two non-adjacent 4h bars** interact with the band (wick or body, at least two bar indices apart, the same counting as before), and the count of interactions is carried on the area. When more candidates qualify than a person would draw, the app keeps the **16 nearest** the current price; bands overlapping an earlier anchor still merge into it. The 60-calendar-day window (about 41 sessions, now roughly 82 four-hour bars) is the app's reading of “five to six weeks”. The tolerance, the minimum of two interactions, the cap and the window are app choices; the recording draws by eye and never retires a line.

### Retest = return to the level, with direction from the leaders and VIX

**Video rule.** “We break and we trade the retest.” The live example is a fade: price just reclaimed the line and he wants a short there if the leaders sell and the VIX buys. Retests are pointed at days after the break.

**App interpretation.** A four-hour-area event still starts on a completed hourly close through the area edge. The **retest** is now any later completed hourly bar that **returns to the area** (its range intersects the band), in either direction; the retest no longer has to close back beyond the edge in the break direction. The trade direction is **not** taken from the break: it is the direction the leader majority and the opposite VIX reaction agree on at confirmation time, which is how the recording's failed-break short (price above the line, leaders selling, VIX buying → short) becomes a recognisable setup. The previous-day sweep method is unchanged and keeps its own retest-free confirmation. The stop formula (beyond the area, origin, confirming and current hourly extremes, minimum 0.1% of entry) and the 1.0 R minimum target excluding the event's own level are unchanged from 4.4.0, including the 4.4 requirement that a target level was established **before the event's origin bar began**.

### Event life to the end of the next session

**Video rule.** No time limit is stated; the retests he shows (October 14 break, retests through October 16–17) are one to three days after the break.

**App interpretation.** A location event now lives from its origin bar to the **end of the next regular session** (two session closes at most), instead of 180 minutes within the origin session. A missing hourly bar between origin and retest still kills the event; an entry still respects the reaction-age, latest-observation and freshness deadlines, and the one-entry-per-event rule is unchanged. Two sessions is an app reading of “days after the break” bounded so that a stale level cannot stay armed for a week.

**Consequences of the longer life, unchanged in this release and listed for the owner's review.** Two 4.4 rules that were harmless within one session behave differently once an event can be entered on the following day. They are recorded here rather than changed, because changing either is a new trading rule that needs its own owner decision:

- *Entry away from the level.* After the retest the event stays confirmed until expiry and the plan is rebuilt on every completed hourly candle with the **latest hourly close** as its entry reference; nothing requires price to still be at the area. The 1% price-movement check is measured against that same latest close, so it does not bound the distance from the level. A plan can therefore become ready hours (or a session) after the retest, several percent from the area, with the stop still beyond the area (a wide stop in R terms, the same $15 purchase) and the 1.0 R target minimum re-checked from the new reference. The recording trades the retest itself.
- *Fewer targets on the next session.* A target must have been established before the origin bar began. For an entry on the session after the break, the previous-day high and low are the break day's, set at its 16:00 close, after the origin, so they are never targets; four-hour areas established after the break are excluded too. Such a setup more often has no level at 1.0 R or more and stays **CONFIRMING** with no plan. This withholds trades; it cannot send a wrong order.

### Leader areas: interaction zones plus period levels, a 4-of-7 majority and a 60-minute reaction window

**Video rule.** Leaders are read on 5-minute (MSFT 15-minute) charts against indicator-plotted period levels and supply/demand boxes; the test is visual (“at supply”, “rejected”, “start seeing selling”); “majority buying → long, majority selling → short, mixed → no trade”; two names are shown, four named; a rejection candle hours old is still cited.

**App interpretation.** Each leader's area set is now the union of its **5-minute repeated-interaction zones** (unchanged) and **period levels** computed from its own data: previous day high, low and close; today's session open; previous week high, low and mid; Monday high and low; the week open; the month open; and the session VWAP (volume-weighted from the session's 5-minute bars). A session inside the seven-session five-minute window takes its open, high, low and close from its regular-session 5-minute candles; only older sessions (the rest of the previous week, the month open) come from Alpaca's daily candles, which are not verified to exclude extended-hours prints. The daily frame is therefore an entry input but not a readiness gate: a failed daily read keeps the last validated daily candles and is reported on the data-health panel, so one failed read cannot change the leader areas between polls; only when no daily candle has ever been read since the worker started are the levels that need it skipped. A period level is a zero-width line treated as an area of its own; a reaction at it uses the same close-beyond inequalities as a zone reaction. The vote is now a **majority of 4 of 7** agreeing with **at most 1 opposing** (was 5 of 7); a conflicting company stays neutral; all seven latest candles must still share one completed timestamp, and a missing candle still blocks. A reaction stays valid for **60 minutes** (was 15) while later closes stay beyond the level in the vote direction; a close back through it still drops the vote. The quorum, the opposing limit, the window and the choice of period levels are app choices; the recording has no count and no window, and its supply/demand boxes are not reproduced (see below).

### VIX consolidation bases beside swing clusters

**Video rule.** No lines on the VIX. “Look left” two to three days on the 15-minute to the consolidation base a spike launched from, and see whether the current close is back at that base and “starts buying”.

**App interpretation.** VIX areas are now the union of the 4.4.0 repeated swing-extreme clusters (±1% of the level, two latest bars excluded) and **consolidation bases**: any run of at least **four** consecutive completed 15-minute candles whose highs and lows all lie within **2%** of each other, before a move away, defines a base whose bounds are the run's low and high. The reaction rule is unchanged (either of the last two completed candles of the current session, opposite the QQQ direction, not later closed back through, fresh actual-index quote before entry). The four-bar minimum and the 2% width are app choices for what he identifies by eye; the 14-calendar-day history covers his “two to three days” with margin.

### Analysis and policy versions

The analysis identifier advances from `nasdaq-video-interpretation-v4` to **`nasdaq-video-interpretation-v5`** and the Socrates policy from `nasdaq-qqq-execution-v6-reward-risk-vix-persistence` to **`nasdaq-qqq-execution-v7-video-aligned`**. Event identities include the analysis version, so no event confirmed under the 4.4 rules can be replayed under the 4.5 rules. The crypto rule (`range-reversal-v2`) and crypto policy (`range-spot-execution-v2`) are unchanged.

## Execution changes

### PSQ inverse-ETF short proxy

**Why.** The account is a cash account that cannot short, and the $15 purchase amount cannot form a whole QQQ share, so every short setup since 4.0.0 has been skipped and reported. Half of the recording's examples — including its live one — are shorts. The owner accepted the instrument translation that 4.4.0 deferred.

**How (app interpretation; the video says only “Nasdaq short”).** A confirmed Socrates **short** is executed as a **long purchase of PSQ**, the −1× daily inverse Nasdaq-100 ETF, for the saved dollar amount (fractional, like a QQQ long). The plan is still computed on QQQ (entry reference, stop beyond structure, 1.0 R target); the stop and target are **translated to PSQ by percentage** — measured from the **live QQQ bid** at admission (not the hourly-close reference, which QQQ may have left by up to the 1% movement allowance), a QQQ stop level *x*% above that bid becomes a PSQ stop *x*% below the PSQ ask, the target likewise, so the PSQ exits sit as far away as QQQ's structural stop and target are from where QQQ trades — and the PSQ order path then applies the same fresh-quote, spread, price-movement, budget, protection and exit rules as a QQQ long. Proxy positions are **intraday only**: they are flattened in the five-minute closing window like every Socrates position, and no proxy position is carried overnight. The trade record names both symbols (`QQQ` setup, `PSQ` instrument), keeps the QQQ bid the distances were measured from (`proxy_geometry.signal_price`), and the dashboard shows the proxy explicitly.

**Risks the owner accepts.** PSQ tracks −1× the daily return of the Nasdaq-100 with tracking error and its own bid/ask, so a QQQ level translated by percentage is approximate on PSQ; the ETF resets daily, so a multi-day hold would drift from the inverse (which is why proxy positions never hold overnight); PSQ has its own quote, so a stale or wide PSQ quote can block an entry the QQQ setup admitted; PSQ liquidity is a fraction of QQQ's. The stop and target are enforced on the PSQ price, not the QQQ price, so a QQQ stop level touched while PSQ has not moved the translated amount does not exit, and vice versa. The [live-execution note](live-execution.md) carries the same statement.

### “Look left” chart panel and `/api/chart`

The owner could not compare the app's levels with his lines because the levels were numbers inside the analysis state. A new read-only **`/api/chart`** endpoint (module `pivot/chart_data.py`) returns the completed QQQ 4h and 1h candles in the window, the established areas with their pivot price, bounds and interaction count, the previous-day levels, the live location events with their state, the leader period levels used in the latest analysis, and the VIX areas the analysis builds from the same candles: 15-minute repeated swing pivots (labelled “VIX 15m repeated pivot”) and consolidation bases (“VIX consolidation base”). The dashboard's **Look left** panel (`pivot/web/chart.mjs`) draws them as candles and horizontal lines the owner can scroll, exactly to compare with the recording. It reads the same snapshot the worker produced; it performs no analysis of its own, sends nothing to the broker and cannot change a permission.

## What is still NOT aligned

- **Alpaca has no futures.** The app trades QQQ (and PSQ for shorts), not NQ; prices, tick sizes and the continuous-contract history differ, and no back-adjustment applies.
- **Regular session only.** Alpaca's IEX feed is filtered to 09:30–16:00 ET. His 4h bars include the afternoon-into-evening and overnight futures action; the app's two buckets per session cannot form levels from extremes that print outside regular hours, and an opening gap replaces what is a continuous move on his chart.
- **No supply/demand boxes.** His leader indicator's blue supply band and orange demand box are not reproduced; period levels and interaction zones stand in for them.
- **No 5-minute VIX.** InsightSentry Free delivers 15-minute actual-VIX candles; his first look at the VIX is a 5-minute view.
- **No weekly or monthly VWAP.** Leader period levels include the session VWAP only; weekly/monthly VWAP and anchored VWAP from his indicator are not computed.
- Stops, targets, size, the 1.0 R minimum, the 4-of-7 quorum, the 60-minute window, the two-session event life and every tolerance remain app choices with no counterpart in the recording.

## Replay evidence

The integrated v5 analyzer was replayed on the same sixty days of QQQ IEX candles (39 evaluated sessions) and twenty sessions of actual VIX used for 4.4.0, without leader data (no sixty-day five-minute leader history is stored offline). These numbers describe rule frequency and geometry, not performance; no fills are simulated.

| Measure | 4.4.0 rules | 4.5.0 rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Four-hour bars per session | 1 (09:30–13:30) | 2 (09:30–13:30 and 13:30–16:00) |
| QQQ areas in play at a checkpoint | up to dozens of 0.1% bands, no cap | median 14, capped at the 16 nearest (swing-pivot candidates, two nonadjacent interactions) |
| Distinct confirming events in 39 sessions | 54 (17 four-hour retests, 37 previous-day sweeps) | 76 (40 four-hour retests, 36 previous-day sweeps); 34 of 39 sessions had at least one |
| Event-direction plans keeping a target | 75 of 108 | 116 of 152 (24 skipped by the 0.1% minimum stop) |
| Median reward-to-risk of kept plans | 1.56 R | 1.46 R |
| VIX gate true at random, long / short (495 bars) | 16.6% / 12.3% | 22.2% / 15.6% (consolidation bases added beside swing clusters) |

The four-hour retest count more than doubles because a retest is now the return to the level rather than a directional close, and because afternoon extremes now form areas. The VIX gate loosens by about five points because a base counts as an area; it remains far below the 4.3 coin-flip rate. Leader period levels, the majority quorum and the sixty-minute window could not be replayed offline and will be observed live through the Look-left panel and the decision records.

## Unchanged safety properties

Durable claim before every POST; one `client_order_id` per attempt and no resubmission of an unknown order; unknown outcomes reconciled by identifier with the 4.3.2 60-second lost-request resolution; exits never compete (stop cancellation confirmed before a sale); the deployment-readiness contract and the `ny-session-two-v1` session-entry protocol identifier the updater checks; the composite deployment permission fingerprint (a changed policy version is exactly what makes the cards require review); per-strategy allowances of two attempts per New York session; strategy-scoped pauses with the optional `PIVOT_ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL` alert; shared cash reservation across engines; one Socrates position at a time (QQQ or PSQ, never both) and one crypto position per selected market; spot long-only crypto with no simulated short; the 15-second submission budget from quote receipt and the 90-second crypto confirmation window.
