        Video alignment · version 4.5Socrates rules in plain wordsOwner controlled
        1. Mark the levels. Four-hour swing highs and lows that price touched at least twice in the last five to six weeks, including the afternoon four-hour candle, plus the previous day’s high and low. The 16 nearest are watched.
        2. Wait for a break and a return. An hourly candle closes through a level, then price comes back to it. The break stays valid until the end of the next session. The entry must be at the level (within 0.4%).
        3. Ask the leaders. At least 4 of the 7 tech leaders react at their own levels in the same direction, at most 1 against, within 60 minutes. Mixed means no trade.
        4. Confirm with the VIX. The actual VIX turns the opposite way at one of its own levels or consolidation bases.
        5. Plan the trade. Stop beyond the level; target at the next level that is at least as far away as the stop (1R minimum). No qualifying target means no trade.
        6. Place it. Longs buy QQQ. Shorts buy PSQ, the inverse Nasdaq-100 ETF, with the stop and target translated by percentage, and close the same day.

        Limits that always apply

        One Socrates position at a time. At most two entry attempts per New York session (rejected or uncertain orders count). No new entries in the last 30 minutes; open positions close 5 minutes before the close. A rejected order pauses Socrates only.

        What the app cannot copy from the video

        Alpaca has no Nasdaq futures, so the app uses QQQ during regular hours only. Leader supply/demand boxes, the 5-minute VIX and weekly/monthly VWAP are not reproduced. Stops, targets, the 4-of-7 count, the 60-minute window and every tolerance are the app’s choices; the videos state none of them. Live fills and profitability have not been established.
